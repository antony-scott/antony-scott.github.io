So you've made a screen recording of your latest and greatest software creation. Now you're staring at it, thinking, *"How do I make a half-decent looking demo of this without learning / paying for Final Cut Pro or selling my soul to Adobe?"*

Enter iMovie, Apple's free, deceptively powerful video editing app. Whether you're a software developer, product manager, or just someone who's never touched a timeline in your life, this guide will get you from complete noob to a polished export, fast.

### **🎬 Before You Start: Tips for Screen Recording**

Got a screen recording? Don’t stress about syncing it perfectly with your voiceover. Just leave a small pause between sections of your demo — it’s all fixable in the edit!

### **📦 What You’ll Need**

- A Mac (any reasonably recent one)
- iMovie (pre-installed or available free on the App Store)
- A screen recording.

## **🛠 Step-by-Step: Edit Your First Video in iMovie**

### **1. Create a New Project**
- Open iMovie.
- Click **“Create New”** → **“Movie.”**

### **2. Import Your Footage & other assets**
- Click **“Import Media”** or drag your screen recording, voiceover audio recordings, background music and overlay images / logos into the media panel.
- Once imported, drag the clip(s) into the timeline at the bottom of the screen.

### **3. Trim the Fat**
- Hover over the beginning or end of any clip in the timeline, be it the screen recording, voice over background music or anything else
- Click and drag to trim — snip off that awkward “is it recording?” moment or the dead space at the end.

### **4. Split & Cut**
- Want to cut something out of the middle?
    - Move the playhead to the spot.
    - Press **⌘ B** to split.
    - Click the unwanted section and press **Delete**.

### **5. Add Titles or Text**
- Click the **“Titles”** tab (top left).
- Drag a style into the timeline above your clip.
- Customise the text in the preview window.
- To adjust a clip’s volume, hover over it in the timeline and look for the thin **horizontal volume line** running across the waveform. 
  - Drag the line **up** to increase volume, or **down** to decrease it.  
  - The adjustment applies to the entire clip.

    ![iMovie audio clip volume](/assets/imovie-audio-clip-volume.gif)

- To fade audio in or out, select the audio (or video) clip. Look for small **fade handles** (circles) at the left and right edges, for video clips these will appear in the lower portion of the clip's area.
  - Drag a handle inward to create a fade — you’ll see a curved line on the clip showing the fade shape.  
  - This is useful for smoothing transitions and removing audible pops at the start or end of a voiceover.
  
    ![iMovie audio clip fade](/assets/imovie-audio-clip-fade.gif)

> 🗣️ _Useful for intros, lower thirds, or adding context to silent screen recordings._


### **6. Add Music or Voiceover (Optional)**
- Click **Audio** to add background music.
- Or hit the mic icon below the preview to record a quick voiceover.

> 🎧 _Be sure to lower music volume if you’re narrating over it._

### **7. Pause for Voiceover**
If you need more time to explain something in your narration, you can “pause” the action in your video by inserting a still frame.  

- **Find the point** in the clip where you want the pause to happen and position the playhead there. This is where those small pauses you incorporated into your screen recording come in to play.
- Right click on the video clip and select **Add Freeze Frame**, once the freeze frame appear in the timeline it can be **stretched** to last as long as your explanation takes.  

![iMovie freeze frame](/assets/imovie-freeze-frame.gif)

> 💡 _This trick lets your voiceover continue while the video “holds” on a frame, making it easier for viewers to follow along._

### **8. Transitions & Polish**
- Use the **“Transitions”** tab to smooth cuts.
- Avoid going overboard — clean cuts are often best.
- You can adjust clip speed, apply filters, or zoom in with the **“Crop to Fill”** tool.

### **9. Export Your Masterpiece**
- Click the **Share** button (top right) → **File**.
- Choose resolution (1080p is a safe bet), quality, and save it.

> 📁 _Your video is now ready to share on Slack, YouTube, or in your next pitch._


## **⚡️ Pro Tips**
- **Undo is your friend:** **⌘ Z** works everywhere.
- **Save often:** iMovie usually auto-saves, but don’t test your luck.
- **Use ⌘+ and ⌘–** to zoom in/out of the timeline.
- **Use “Detach Audio”** on clips to delete or move background noise.

## **🎉 You Did It!**
Editing video might seem like magic, but with iMovie, it’s surprisingly learnable. In under 30 minutes, you can go from raw footage to a cleaned-up, watchable video — no course, no crash, no stress.
